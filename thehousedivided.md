# THE TRAGEDY OF ABRAHAM LINCOLN
## *or, The House Divided*

*A new play in five acts, written after the manner of William Shakespeare.*  

---

## DRAMATIS PERSONAE

**Of the North:**  

- ABRAHAM LINCOLN, *President of the United States*  
- MARY TODD LINCOLN, *his wife*  
- WILLIAM SEWARD, *Secretary of State*  
- EDWIN STANTON, *Secretary of War*  
- GEORGE McCLELLAN, *a general, much given to delay*  
- ULYSSES GRANT, *a general, much given to none*  
- FREDERICK DOUGLASS, *an orator, sometime held in bondage*  
- MAJOR RATHBONE, *a soldier*  

**Of the South:**  

- JEFFERSON DAVIS, *President of the Confederate States*  
- ROBERT E. LEE, *a general of Virginia*  
- THOMAS JACKSON, *called STONEWALL, his lieutenant*  
- JAMES LONGSTREET, *his lieutenant*  
- PIERRE BEAUREGARD, *a general before Charleston*  
- EDMUND RUFFIN, *an old fire-eater of Virginia*  

**Of the common sort:**  

- ASA DRUMMOND, *a farmer of Kentucky, after a soldier of the Union*  
- CALEB DRUMMOND, *his younger brother, after a soldier of the Confederacy*  
- WIDOW DRUMMOND, *their mother*  
- CATO, *a man enslaved upon the Calhoun place, after called FREEMAN, a sergeant of the United States Colored Troops*  
- BARNABY POTTLE, *a sutler, a great coward and a greater talker*  
- A LIEUTENANT of South Carolina  

**Of the conspiracy:**  

- JOHN WILKES BOOTH, *an actor*  
- LEWIS POWELL, *his creature*  
- GEORGE ATZERODT, *a ferryman, faint of heart*  
- DAVID HEROLD, *a boy*  

**CHORUS.**  

*Ghosts, senators, soldiers of both armies, pickets, surgeons, messengers, a parson, players, attendants.*  

THE SCENE: *the United States and the states in rebellion; four years of war.*  

---

## PROLOGUE

*Enter CHORUS.*  

**CHORUS.**  
Two houses, of one blood and of one birth,  
Both rocked within one cradle of a war  
That won them liberty, yet bound the earth  
With chains they swore to think upon no more:  
Behold them now, the children of one bed,  
That share one tongue, one scripture, and one shore,  
Set at their common table blade and lead,  
And carve their mother's body on the board.  
O pardon, gentles, that our shadowed stage  
Would press four bleeding years in three poor hours;  
No theatre's throat can swallow Gettysburg,  
Nor any painted thunder counterfeit  
The guns that broke the morning at Bull Run.  
Yet thoughts are armies: let your working minds  
Supply our muster; when we name a field,  
See half a hundred thousand in the smoke;  
And when one brother strikes his brother down,  
Think then upon six hundred thousand graves.  
A tall man walks before us through the dark,  
Sad-eyed, and stooping with the general woe,  
Who bears the Union as a father bears  
A fevered child through watches of the night.  
How he did hold the house, and mend the house,  
And pay the mending with his proper blood,  
Our play presents. Judge gently of our art:  
The wound was real; we show you but the scar.  

*Exit.*

# ACT I

## SCENE I. Charleston. The Battery, before dawn.

*Enter BEAUREGARD, RUFFIN, a LIEUTENANT, with officers and soldiers.*  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
The fourth hour of the morning, and no word.  
Yon fortress squats upon the harbour's throat  
Even as a bone stuck in a swallowing gullet,  
And Sumter is its name, and in its walls  
A stubborn major and fourscore hungry men  
Hold up one banner that offends the dawn.  

**RUFFIN.**  
Offends the dawn! It hath offended me  
These thirty years. I have grown white in waiting,  
General, white and bent; I cried secession  
When these young gallants hung on leading-strings,  
And was called madman for my morning news.  
Well: madmen and the weather change together,  
And mine is now the sanity in fashion.  
Give me a gun to fire, and I'll die happy.  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
Old man, thy hunger does thee little grace.  

**LIEUTENANT.**  
My general, may a young man speak his doubt?  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
Speak it, but briefly; doubt is a long sermon.  

**LIEUTENANT.**  
He that shall fire the first gun of this war  
Charges the piece with more than serge and powder:  
He rams a generation down the bore.  
The ball we loose this morning will not fall;  
It will go up, and up, and hang in the heaven  
Above both houses like a second moon,  
And rain for years. Sir, are we sure the cause  
Will bear the weight of all that it must bury?  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
The time is past for surety, lieutenant.  
I learnt my gunnery of a Yankee master  
At the Point, in the old army, the one army—  
Major Anderson; he taught my hand its trade;  
And now my lesson must be sent him home  
Sealed in a shell. There is the civil war:  
The teacher in the fort, the scholar shooting,  
And both of them with tears behind their eyes.  

*Enter a MESSENGER.*  

**MESSENGER.**  
From Major Anderson, sir, his compliments,  
And he will not strike his flag.  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
                Why, there's an end.  
He answers like the soldier that he is,  
And we must ask again with iron tongues.  
Lieutenant, to your battery. Note the hour:  
Half after four. The morning star is up.  

**RUFFIN.**  
And by your leave, the lanyard, sir, to me;  
Let the first thunder wear an old man's hand.  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
Take it; and God forgive us all this day.  

*A gun sounds. The sky reddens. All look toward the harbour.*  

**LIEUTENANT** *(aside)*.  
So. It is loosed, and cannot be recalled.  
The very gulls cry havoc round the fort,  
And yonder, where the smoke goes up like prayer,  
The Union, that our grandsires glued with blood,  
Cracks down the middle like a frozen river.  
What ice we walk on now, God only knows.  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
Open with all the batteries! Give it breath!  
Now, Sumter, we shall ask thee in a voice  
That Washington shall hear, and London too.  

**RUFFIN.**  
Mark how she burns! O, I could weep for joy.  

**LIEUTENANT** *(aside)*.  
And I for grief; and ere this work be done,  
Joy shall be rarer than a winter rose,  
And grief as common as the morning bread.  

**BEAUREGARD.**  
Cease not till she has struck. Come, gentlemen:  
This day we are made new, or quite unmade;  
The dice are thrown that cannot be unplayed.  

*Exeunt. Guns continue within.*  

---

## SCENE II. Washington. A room in the White House.

*Enter LINCOLN, with papers, and SEWARD.*  

**SEWARD.**  
Sir, yet again I press it: give them Sumter.  
A fort is stones; the Union is a soul;  
Trade stones for time, and time may save the soul.  
One April month of patience and soft words—  

**LINCOLN.**  
Soft words. I have been soft these many weeks,  
Seward, as soft as April; and the South  
Takes all my mildness for a coward's stammer.  
I told them at the swearing of my oath:  
In *your* hands, not in mine, my sundered countrymen,  
Rests the great question of this civil war.  
The government will not assail you. None.  
You have no oath in heaven to destroy it;  
I have the most solemn one to keep and guard it.  
I cannot barter forts to buy good weather.  
Give them this fort, they'll ask the next, and next,  
Till all the coast is theirs, and the flag a guest  
In its own house, that begs to keep its room.  

**SEWARD.**  
Then you will hold it?  

**LINCOLN.**  
                I will *feed* it, Seward.  
Bread, mark you: bread, no powder and no men.  
I'll send them loaves, and tell them loaves are coming.  
If they fire then, they fire upon plain bread,  
And all the world shall see whose gun spoke first.  

**SEWARD.**  
That is a lawyer's trick.  

**LINCOLN.**  
                It is a lawyer's *prayer*.  
I pray they let the bread go through, and choose  
The slow, sure, bloodless verdict of the years.  
But if they shoot—  

*Enter STANTON with a paper, hastily.*  

**STANTON.**  
        They have shot, sir. It is done.  
The wires from Charleston: yesterday at dawn  
Their batteries opened on the fort, and beat it  
A day and night about the ears, and now  
Anderson strikes his colours, and marches out  
With drums, saluting the rag they made him lower.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Was any man slain?  

**STANTON.**  
        None in all the storm—  
A mercy that will never come again.  

**LINCOLN.**  
No, never again. The first gun kills a country  
And not one man; the rest kill men enough.  
Stanton, the proclamation. Seward, write:  
I call upon the states for volunteers,  
Militia, seventy-five thousand strong,  
To serve three months.  

**SEWARD.**  
        Three months, sir, will it serve?  

**LINCOLN.**  
We'll say three months. A man that breaks his leg  
Is told it is a sprain, to get him home.  
Go, gentlemen. The bells will ring tonight  
In Charleston; let them ring. Bells ring for weddings,  
And ring the same for fires. Leave me a while.  

*Exeunt SEWARD and STANTON.*  

So. It begins; and not as argument,  
Whereof I have had bellyfuls in court,  
But iron: the long sentence of the guns,  
Whose grammar is the grave. I said it once  
In Springfield, and they cawed me for a crow  
That croaks of storm in harvest: that a house  
Divided 'gainst itself can never stand.  
I did not think to be the carpenter  
That proves the saying with his bleeding hands.  
This Union is my father's house, and theirs,  
The very men that burn it; mad inheritance,  
Where half the heirs make bonfire of the roof-tree  
Because the other half would douse the flame  
That cooks their dinner—and O, there's the meat of it,  
The dinner: four millions of living souls  
Bought, bred, and butchered like the autumn hogs,  
And called by law a *property*, like rain-barrels.  
If slavery be not wrong, then nothing's wrong;  
And yet I am sworn not to put out that fire  
Where law has fenced it, only pen it in—  
A pretty oath: to watch the serpent nurse  
And only stay its travels. Well. The oath  
Is now the lighter half my burden. War  
Is come, that great unsettler of all bargains.  
I must be patient, subtle, slow, and sure;  
Hold the border states as a man holds eggs;  
Find generals; find money; find the words  
To make a ploughboy die for an abstraction.  
They say I am a simple western man,  
And so I am; and rail-splitters know one thing  
The scholars do not—nay, plain, man, keep it plain:  
We know which way the grain runs in the wood,  
And where to set the wedge. God set me right,  
For here I split, or am split. To the work.  

*Exit.*  

---

## SCENE III. Arlington. LEE'S house upon the hill, by night. The lights of Washington seen across the river.

*Enter LEE, with two letters.*  

**LEE.**  
Two letters, and one hand to write with. Here,  
The old flag's offer: the command of all  
The armies of the Union, mine, at a word—  
The summit of a soldier's climbing life,  
Thirty years' service rolled into a star.  
And here, Virginia, calling home her son  
With a mother's voice, that hath no argument  
But only that she is my mother. God,  
That made me of this clay and of this river,  
Why hast Thou set my honours at such war  
That I must be a traitor either way—  
To the broad land I served, or the dear ground  
That serves me at my table, holds my dead?  
Below me sleep the lamplights of the law,  
The capital, white as a catechism;  
And at my back, southward, the dark fields breathe  
Where I was got, and where my children play.  
I am two men this night, and ere the cock,  
One of the two must take the other's life.  
Secession is rebellion; I have said it;  
There is no sweeter word will make it sweet.  
And slavery—I'll juggle not with that:  
Whatever men contrive to call that thing,  
It sits not easy at my evening prayers.  
And yet, to draw my sword on mine own blood,  
On Norfolk, on the Shenandoah farms,  
On boys I christened—how, upon the day,  
When the guns level, and I see the hills  
Of my own country in the gun-sights—how?  
A man may love a roof and love a hearth;  
The Union is a roof, most excellent  
Against all weather of the world; but Virginia,  
Virginia is the fire upon the hearth,  
And I am cold, and old, and must go in.  

*He sits, and writes.*  

"To the honourable Secretary: sir, I tender  
The resignation of my commission—" so.  
Three lines, to bury thirty years. 'Tis done.  
*(He seals it.)*  
Now, save in the defence of mine own state,  
I hope I may not ever draw this sword.  
—I hear the hope crack even as I speak it.  
Roof, I have loved thee; hearth, I come to thee;  
And if the choosing damn me, as it may,  
Let it be writ I chose not for ambition,  
Nor hate, nor profit, but for love alone—  
Love, that excuses nothing, but explains.  
Lights of the city, burn; I shall not see you  
Again, I think, but over trenched fields  
And through the smoke. Virginia, I am thine.  

*Exit.*  

---

## SCENE IV. Kentucky. The Drummond farm.

*Enter ASA and CALEB DRUMMOND, from the field; CATO, with a scythe, following.*  

**CALEB.** I tell thee, Asa, the thing is simple as a fence-rail: a state came into the Union of her own free legs, and her own free legs may carry her out again. That is all the law of it.  

**ASA.** And I tell thee the thing is simple as a wedding: thy legs carry thee *in* of thine own will, brother, but they do not carry thee out whenever the dinner displeases thee. Thou hast sworn something. The whole is greater than the leg.  

**CALEB.** Hark at the philosopher! He reads one winter with the parson and comes home Daniel Webster.  

**ASA.** Better one winter with the parson than every summer with the Calhoun boys, drinking their cider and spouting their cotton catechism. What is South Carolina to thee? Thou hast never seen salt water, nor owned so much as a share in a mule.  

**CALEB.** It is not cotton, it is *liberty*—  

**ASA.** Liberty! Cato, come hither. Caleb, look on him. There stands liberty's bill of sale. Ask Cato what liberty is, that mows old Calhoun's hay at the rope's end and sees his wife sold off to Tennessee like a heifer—  

**CALEB.** I own no man! Pa never owned a man, nor his pa neither!  

**ASA.** No, but thou wilt die in a ditch that other men may own men; that is thy "liberty," and there is no other in the bottom of it, scrub the barrel how thou wilt.  

**CATO.** Young masters, by your leave, the hay won't quarrel; it wants only mowing.  

**CALEB.** Thou hear him? Cato is content.  

**CATO.** Master Caleb, the fish in the pail is quiet too. Quiet ain't content.  

**ASA.** *(laughing bitterly)* There is thy answer, with a scythe to point it.  

**CALEB.** I'll hear no more. Tom Calhoun rides for Tennessee tonight, to the mustering, and I ride with him. There's a regiment forming under a Virginia colonel, and gray cloth and glory in it.  

**ASA.** Then I'll tell thee what I had hoped to bury: there's a Union camp over the river by Cincinnati, and blue cloth in it, and I had thought to go alone and spare Mother the both of us. Now she loses two by the same lightning.  

*Enter WIDOW DRUMMOND.*  

**WIDOW.** What is this talk of losing? I heard "mother" used as men use it when they mean to break her.  

**CALEB.** Mother—  

**WIDOW.** Nay, I see it on the both of you, like fever on a face. One north, one south. I have read of such in scripture, Cain and Abel, and I did pray Kentucky might be Eden enough to keep you. Hear me, then, since hearing is all that is left a woman: I curse neither cause, for I understand neither, and I think no man in a fine coat understands them honestly either. But you are brothers before you are soldiers. Swear me one thing: whatever field you stand on, you will remember you drank of one milk.  

**ASA.** I swear it, Mother.  

**CALEB.** And I. *(aside)* And yet they say the milk is soonest forgot when the powder speaks.  

**WIDOW.** Then go, the pair of you, before my courage goes first. Cato—bid Cato bring the lantern; it is getting dark earlier than it used.  

*Exeunt WIDOW, ASA, and CALEB, severally.*  

**CATO.**  
It gets no darker, mistress; dark's the same;  
It is your eyes that alter, not the evening.  
*(He looks after them.)*  
So. They will quarrel who shall keep the door,  
The while the man locked in the cellar listens.  
North wind and south wind fight about the house;  
The fire in the cellar knows its hour.  
They say in meeting, softly, out of the dark:  
There is a war come walking up the road,  
And in its knapsack, wrapped in bloody linen,  
A thing addressed to us, that no man yet  
Has dared to read aloud. I can be patient.  
I have been patient as the dead are patient,  
And watched, as graves watch, everything that passes.  
Go, brothers, ride; ride north and south together;  
Whichever of you falls, the chain links rattle;  
And Cato prays for neither, and for both,  
And for himself a road, a name, a battle.  

*Exit with the lantern.*

# ACT II

## SCENE I. A road near Manassas, in Virginia. Evening, after the battle of Bull Run.

*Alarums afar off. Enter BARNABY POTTLE, a sutler, running, laden with hampers; after him certain SOLDIERS in disorder, ASA DRUMMOND among them.*  

**POTTLE.** Make way! Make way for the commissary! War is no place for a man of business!  

**FIRST SOLDIER.** Look where the picnic runs! Hold, fat-pockets—what news ahead?  

**POTTLE.** News? The news is behind me, friend, and I will not stay for the second edition. The whole Confederacy is back yonder, horse, foot, and artillery, and every man of them nine feet high. I saw a regiment of black horse ride down a senator's barouche and eat the senator's cold chicken, and the senator was glad to compound for the chicken.  

**ASA.** Thou never stood near enough the fighting to smell it, sutler. Thy nine-foot rebels were dust and thine own conscience.  

**POTTLE.** Smell it! I am a sutler, sir; my nose is my livelihood, and I keep it out of pawn. I came out from Washington with the carriages and the parasols, to sell lemonade at the famous victory—half the Congress came in gigs to see the war end in an afternoon, with opera-glasses.  

**SECOND SOLDIER.** And now Congress, parasols, lemonade, and army are all one current flowing back to Washington, and the devil take the rearmost.  

**ASA.** It was not all running. The regulars stood; and there was a Virginia brigade on the hill stood like a stone wall, and we broke on it from noon till four. Whoever set them there knew his trade. I tell you, boys, this war hath changed its size today. I came out a three-months man; I am going back a three-years one.  

**POTTLE.** And I came out with four hampers and go back with two, and the firm of Pottle is with the army till the army pays its account. Misery, gentlemen, is a great opener of purses; I foresee a long and prosperous war. Buy a ginger-cake before despair raises the price?  

**FIRST SOLDIER.** Hang thy ginger-cakes.  

**POTTLE.** They have hanged better men. Sixpence.  

**ASA.** Give him his sixpence; even the crow must live that follows the plough. *(aside)* And O, what a ploughing is begun: the share is iron, and the furrow runs from here clean back to my mother's door; and what is sown in it, God keep me from reaping. Caleb, art thou yonder on the hill tonight? They say the Kentucky boys were with the stone wall. I'll not think on it. A soldier's first lesson: load, aim, and do not think.  

*Drums beat. Exeunt.*  

---

## SCENE II. Washington. The White House.

*Enter LINCOLN and STANTON, to them McCLELLAN.*  

**McCLELLAN.**  
You sent for me, sir.  

**LINCOLN.**  
        I did, and do, and have,  
General; my messengers wear out their boots.  
You have the grandest army of this earth,  
A hundred thousand men, drilled to a hair,  
Fed, horsed, and harnessed; it is autumn now;  
I ask but this: when will it move?  

**McCLELLAN.**  
                It moves  
When it is ready, sir, and not before.  
An army is an instrument; ill-tuned,  
It plays but funerals. The foe outnumbers me—  

**STANTON.**  
Outnumbers! By the count of your own spies,  
Who'd find three rebels hiding in one coat.  
You ask for men; we send them; still you ask;  
Sir, if we mustered in the cherubim  
You'd hold the seraphim were ranged against you.  

**McCLELLAN.**  
I am not used, sir, to be school'd by clerks.  

**STANTON.**  
Nor I to buy a racehorse that won't run.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Gently, good Stanton. General, hear me out.  
I am a patient man; I can hold a horse  
While it eats grass through six months of fine weather;  
But there's a country looking on, that bleeds  
At every day's expense and no day's deed,  
And over the water, England, like a glover,  
Measuring the South's hand for recognition.  
The army is your instrument, you say:  
Why, so; but it is not your *property*.  
It is the people's; I but hold it for them;  
And if General McClellan has no use for it,  
I should be glad to borrow it a while.  

**McCLELLAN.**  
Sir, this is wit, and war is not a witticism.  

**LINCOLN.**  
No, it is arithmetic, and of the dourest.  
Yonder is Richmond; here stand you; between,  
An army smaller than your own. March, fight,  
And losing, fight again; for we can lose  
The selfsame battle four times over, general,  
And mend; let Lee lose once entire, he is bankrupt.  
That is the dismal sum of this whole war,  
And every week you drill it is a week  
The dying is deferred but not diminished.  

**McCLELLAN.**  
When I am ready, sir, I'll end this war  
At one stroke, scientifically, whole;  
I will not peddle out my soldiers' blood  
By the spoonful, to sweeten editorials.  

**LINCOLN.**  
I ask no spoonfuls; I ask only *motion*.  
Well. Go your ways tonight; think on't; good evening.  

*Exit McCLELLAN.*  

**STANTON.**  
He will not fight, sir. He has every gift—  
The eye, the head, the love of all his men—  
Every gift but the stomach. Cashier him.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Not yet. He builds well, though he will not sail.  
I'd bear his insolence on my own back  
Gladly, and hold his stirrup in the street,  
If he would only win us victories.  
But you say true, I fear: he loves the army  
Too tenderly to spend it; and a general  
Must be a kind of spendthrift in his heart,  
And after, weep. Find me a weeping spendthrift,  
Stanton, and I will make him my commander  
Though he drink whiskey and wear last year's coat.  

**STANTON.**  
There's one out west, sir, took two forts last winter—  
A quiet, seedy, cigar-smoking fellow;  
When he was asked his terms at Donelson,  
He answered: none but unconditional surrender.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Unconditional surrender. Mark the man;  
Send me his news; I like a short vocabulary.  
Come, to the war-maps; we two amateurs  
Must play at generals till a general comes,  
And heaven mend our blunders with the sums.  

*Exeunt.*

## SCENE III. Richmond. A chamber of the Confederate White House.

*Enter DAVIS, LEE, and JACKSON, with maps.*  

**DAVIS.**  
They press us, gentlemen, on every coast;  
Their fleets shut up our harbours, door by door;  
The west leaks forts; and on the lower river  
New Orleans, our queen city, wears their flag.  
Money we have but little; powder, less;  
And our best crop is courage, which buys nothing  
Abroad, however much it spends at home.  

**LEE.**  
Then we must make our poverty a weapon.  
We cannot match them gun for gun, nor man  
For man, nor month for month: long war undoes us.  
Therefore strike soon, strike hard, and strike up north;  
Carry the war out of Virginia's garden  
Into their own; make Pennsylvania feel  
The iron housekeeping she sends to us.  
A victory on their soil is worth, abroad,  
Ten on our own; let London read of one  
And recognition follows with the mails.  

**JACKSON.**  
War means fighting, and fighting means killing; sir,  
I am a deacon, and I say it plainly:  
Draw the sword, throw the scabbard in the river,  
And let our drums teach Washington theology.  
Give me ten thousand men this day fortnight,  
And I will dance such measures down the Valley,  
Their generals shall write letters to each other  
Asking which way is north.  

**DAVIS.**  
        General Jackson,  
Your sermons have a smell of powder to them  
I find more comfortable than my treasury.  
Go to your Valley; you, my lord of Arlington—  
Forgive me: of the army—you have heard  
McClellan creeps upon us up the Peninsula.  

**LEE.**  
Creeps is the word; and creeping things are crushed.  
He thinks one battle, lost, would lose him all,  
And so will never venture all on one;  
I'll venture all on every one. Audacity,  
Audacity is the cheap weapon of the poor,  
And we will be the most audacious beggars  
That ever picked a rich man's pocket twice.  

**DAVIS.**  
Then to your armies, gentlemen, and God—  

**JACKSON.**  
—Is just, and being just, must judge our cause—  

**LEE** *(aside)*.  
Or judge our hearts, which is a harder court.  

**DAVIS.**  
We'll trust Him, and keep dry our powder too.  
The South is one year old; pray, gentlemen,  
That what was born in thunder, thrive in it,  
Or find at least an honourable grave.  

**LEE.**  
Graves we shall have at wholesale, win or lose;  
The honour, sir, is all that's left to choose.  

*Exeunt.*

## SCENE IV. Winter. The banks of the Rappahannock; the two camps on either side. Night.

*Enter ASA, as a Union picket, with a fire; on the other side CALEB, a Confederate picket, with another.*  

**ASA.** Cold work, watching a river to see it does not desert.  

**CALEB** *(calling across)*. Hallo, Yank! Is thy coffee real coffee?  

**ASA.** Real as sin. Is thy tobacco real tobacco?  

**CALEB.** Virginia leaf, fit for a bishop. What say you—the old trade? There's a toy boat here the last picket left, sails and all.  

**ASA.** Load her up and shove her over; the tide of war sets my way tonight.  

*The little boat is sent across with tobacco, and returned with coffee.*  

**CALEB.** A fair exchange, and the only honest commerce of this war. Hark ye, Yank, thy voice has a homely sound. What state?  

**ASA.** Kentucky, by Ohio out of necessity.  

**CALEB.** Kentucky! What county, in God's name?  

**ASA.** Bourbon county, near the Calhoun place, by Stoner creek—  

**CALEB.** *(after a silence)* Asa.  

**ASA.** ...Caleb. *(aside)* Now, heart, stand to thy post.  

**CALEB.** Is it well with thee, brother?  

**ASA.** It is well enough with my body. And thee? Art thou fed? They say ye are barefoot over there.  

**CALEB.** They say more than their prayers. I have boots—*(aside)* of a dead man, but he was a tidy size—and I am well, and Mother's last letter found me at Fredericksburg.  

**ASA.** And me the same week. She writes the north field lies fallow, with none to plough it.  

**CALEB.** Aye. *(a pause)* Asa, dost remember the swimming-hole below the mill, and the heron we could never catch?  

**ASA.** I remember all of it, Caleb. That is my trouble of nights: I am a great rememberer.  

*A band strikes up afar off in the Union camp; another answers from the Confederate side; then both fall to one tune together.*  

**CALEB.** Hark, thy band.  

**ASA.** And thine answering. Every night they quarrel so, tune for tune, like cocks crowing.  

**CALEB.** They quarrel not now. Listen—both bands—they are playing the one song together.  

**ASA.**  
'Tis "Home, Sweet Home." Listen how still it falls;  
Two armies, and no cough in all that choir.  

**CALEB.**  
A hundred thousand men, and every man  
Stood by his fire, thinking of one door.  
Brother, if bands commanded the two armies,  
We all were home by Easter.  

**ASA.**  
        But they do not.  
The bands obey the drums, the drums the guns;  
And in the morning, thee and I are soldiers,  
And this most gentle river is a frontier,  
And that toy boat the only vessel sails it  
Without a flag. Caleb: the oath to Mother—  

**CALEB.**  
I keep it nightly, like my other prayers.  
But Asa, hear me, for the fires burn low:  
If it should come, some smoking afternoon,  
That we two meet where there's no river between—  

**ASA.**  
I'll know thee, and not fire.  

**CALEB.**  
        Thou wilt not know me.  
That is the horror of it, brother: smoke,  
And fifty paces, and a stranger's coat—  
Thou'lt know me after.  

**ASA.**  
        God forbid it. Hark,  
The relief comes. Goodnight, Caleb. Keep warm.  

**CALEB.**  
Goodnight, Asa. Drink thy coffee to me.  

**ASA.**  
And thou, smoke my tobacco. Brother—  

**CALEB.**  
                Aye?  

**ASA.**  
Nothing. The word was all. Goodnight, goodnight;  
Two fires; one river; and one home from sight.  

*Exeunt severally. The music dies.*

# ACT III

## SCENE I. Washington. The Cabinet room.

*Enter LINCOLN, with a paper; SEWARD and STANTON attending.*  

**LINCOLN.**  
Sit, gentlemen. I have not called you hither  
To ask your counsel on the main, for that  
I have resolved alone, and answer for  
Alone, to God and to the autumn elections;  
But on the manner, and the season, speak.  
*(He reads.)*  
"That on the first of January next,  
All persons held as slaves in any state  
Then in rebellion, shall be then, thenceforward,  
And forever, free."  

**STANTON.**  
        It is a thunderbolt.  
Loose it today.  

**SEWARD.**  
        It is a thunderbolt,  
And therefore, sir, loose it from some high heaven,  
And not from out a ditch. Our arms go backward;  
McClellan beaten from before their Richmond;  
Loose it this week, all Europe reads it thus:  
The wolf, that could not pull the bullock down,  
Now sets the bullock's stable upon fire—  
The last shriek of a government in retreat.  
Wait, sir; but wait the victory; then proclaim,  
And it shall ride upon the cannon's voice,  
And sound like strength, which is the only language  
The chancelleries of Europe stoop to hear.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Why, there spoke statecraft, and I'll own it wisdom.  
I'll put the paper in this drawer, like grain  
Held over winter; but I tell you, sirs,  
The seed is sound, and it will out in spring,  
For I have made a covenant of it. *(aside)*  
A covenant: I, that quibble at the pulpit,  
Have made a bargain with the God of battles  
Like any pedlar: grant the field, and I sign.  
Strange market for a doubter; but the times  
Drive every man to dealings past his creed.  

*Enter a MESSENGER.*  

**MESSENGER.**  
Sir, from Maryland: a battle by Antietam creek,  
The bloodiest single day this war hath dyed;  
Lee's army, beaten back across Potomac,  
Retires into Virginia.  

**LINCOLN.**  
        And McClellan?  

**MESSENGER.**  
Stands on the field, and does not follow, sir.  

**LINCOLN.**  
He has the slows; God help him, and dismiss him.  
But it will serve. The bullock's down on one knee;  
Now, Seward, comes the thunder from on high.  
The drawer, there. Give me the paper out.  

*Enter FREDERICK DOUGLASS, ushered.*  

And here is come my conscience, walking upright.  
Mr. Douglass, you are welcome; you once said  
My pace would shame a glacier.  

**DOUGLASS.**  
        I said, and say:  
Yet glaciers, sir, have carved out continents,  
And I am come to watch the carving done.  
But let me speak, not for myself alone:  
I speak for four millions that have no speaker,  
Whose backs are ledgers where this nation's sin  
Is writ in such a hand as God can read.  
You free today the slaves of the rebellion;  
Good: it is much; it is not yet enough.  
A man half-freed is half a slave, and slavery,  
Like to the wounded snake, will knit and strike  
Unless the law's heel grind it past all knitting.  
And one thing more: open the army's gate.  
Give the black man a musket and a flag;  
For he who fights for liberty beneath it  
Has earned a deed to it no court can void,  
And there is no such argument in nature  
As a brass eagle on a sable breast.  

**LINCOLN.**  
You shall have muskets. I was slow to it—  
I own my slowness; I have herded always  
A skittish team, the border states, and feared  
One sudden motion overturns the wagon.  
But I am past such fearing. Mr. Douglass,  
I have been called a tyrant for this paper,  
And called a trimmer for its long delay;  
I am content to be called anything,  
So the thing's done. Look you: my hand is stiff—  
I have shaken hands three hours at the levee—  
And if my signature do shake, men will say  
"He hesitated." Wait, then, till the hand  
Remembers it is servant to the heart.  
*(He signs.)*  
There. If my name go ever into history,  
It goes for this, and my whole soul is in it.  
If my soul be in any act of mine,  
It is in this act. Now let the wires sing,  
And ring the bells in every steeple. Stanton,  
See it proclaimed at the head of all the armies.  

**DOUGLASS.**  
Sir, with that pen-stroke, henceforth and forever,  
Your war is no more property's nor pride's,  
But Heaven's own quarrel; and the dead of it  
Die in the oldest service of the world,  
That Moses opened. See you keep the faith;  
For we shall hold you to it, sir—and history,  
That stern recording angel, after us.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Hold me to it; I ask no gentler holding.  
The promise being made, must now be kept;  
In giving freedom to the slave, we assure it  
To the free: honourable alike in what  
We give and what preserve. Gentlemen, the world  
Will little note what's spoken in this room,  
But it can never quite unhear that bell.  
A new year, and a jubilee. To work;  
For paper freedom must be ratified  
In a hard court, whose judges sit in batteries,  
And bayonets are the clerks that copy it fair.  

*Exeunt.*  

---

## SCENE II. The Wilderness near Chancellorsville. Night, after victory.

*Alarums afar. Enter JACKSON, with two AIDES, in the dark wood.*  

**JACKSON.**  
Press on, press on; the beaten enemy  
Hangs like a broken bough, and one more shake  
Brings all down. I must see their lines myself.  
The night is friendly; night was ever friendly  
To us; we have marched further in the dark  
Than they in daylight.  

**FIRST AIDE.**  
        General, have a care;  
We are past our pickets, and the woods are nervous;  
A twig's crack hath a regiment's imagination.  

**JACKSON.**  
The enemy is yonder, friend, not here.  
Old Hooker's host, a hundred thousand strong,  
Folded like sheep at evening—and by what?  
By marching, marching; by the feet, the feet;  
My foot-cavalry won this field at noon  
Or ever a musket spoke. Come, to the front;  
Tomorrow we shall press them to the river,  
And then, please God—  

*A volley within, from their own lines.*  

**FIRST AIDE.**  
        Cease firing! Cease! You fire  
Upon your own! 'Tis General Jackson! Cease!  

*JACKSON falls, shot. The aides bear him up.*  

**JACKSON.**  
My own men. Tell them—nay, tell them nothing.  
They did as I have taught them: shoot the dark,  
And ask the dark its name when it is down.  
My arm. Both. The left is broken, wing and bone.  

**SECOND AIDE.**  
A litter here! And surgeons!  

**JACKSON.**  
        Make no outcry;  
Let not the soldiers know who has been hit;  
An army's heart should never see its own blood.  

*He is laid down. Enter a SURGEON; the scene closes to a tent; time passes.*  

**SURGEON.**  
The arm is off; he rallied; now the lung  
Takes fever to it, and I fear the flood  
Is past my little dykes. He wanders much.  

*Enter an OFFICER with a letter.*  

**OFFICER.**  
From General Lee: "Give him my warmest thanks;  
He has lost his left arm; I have lost my right."  

**JACKSON** *(waking)*.  
Who speaks of loss? The day was wholly won...  
Order A. P. Hill to prepare for action...  
Pass the infantry to the front... tell Major Hawks—  
*(He pauses; his voice changes.)*  
No, no; let us pass over; it is Sunday;  
I always wished to die upon a Sunday.  
The week's work done, and all the camp at prayers.  
How strangely quiet now... the guns have stopped...  
Let us cross over the river, and rest there,  
Under the shade of the trees.  

*He dies.*  

**SURGEON.**  
        He's crossed indeed,  
Where no flank-march of ours can follow him.  

**OFFICER.**  
Then mourn, Virginia; this one tent tonight  
Holds more of thy hard fortune than thou knowest.  
Great pillars make great ruins when they fall;  
And the south wall is fallen. Bear him hence;  
Muffle the drums along the whole army's length;  
And he that won by marching, slow may ride.  
Heaven's was the soldier; ours, only the pride.  

*Exeunt, bearing the body. A dead march.*  

---

## SCENE III. Gettysburg. The third day.

*Enter LEE and LONGSTREET, upon Seminary Ridge; the Union line seen far off upon the opposite ridge.*  

**LONGSTREET.**  
General, look long upon that ridge before  
You bid men walk to it. A mile of open,  
Fenced, and uphill, and at the top of it  
Their guns stand wheel to wheel. I have been a soldier,  
Man and boy, in every kind of quarrel,  
And by my judgment no fifteen thousand men  
Ever arrayed for battle can take that hill.  

**LEE.**  
And by my judgment, they can, and they will.  
Two days we have bent both ends of their long line;  
The middle, therefore, is drawn thin as Lent.  
The enemy is there, and there I strike him.  
Longstreet, we cannot siege, and cannot stay:  
This army never feeds two weeks together  
On hostile ground; it conquers or it starves.  
Yonder's the Union centre; one clean blow,  
And the war ends, and all the dying with it—  
The shortest road home runs across that field.  

**LONGSTREET.**  
Or into it. Sir, I have said my say;  
It is your order; I will give it; and may God  
Be kinder to those boys than arithmetic.  

**LEE.**  
The fault, if fault there be, on me, all me.  
Sound for the cannonade. And gentlemen—  
Virginians—forward, in the name of home.  

*Exeunt. Alarums; a great cannonade; then the charge, with smoke and cries. Enter, at the stone wall, ASA with soldiers, defending; the Confederates come on; among them CALEB. They fight pell-mell in the smoke. ASA and a CONFEDERATE SOLDIER fight singly; ASA runs him through; the rest sway off stage fighting.*  

**ASA.**  
Down, rebel, down; and stay; thy charge is done.  
*(The field clears about them; cries afar off.)*  
They break; the wave breaks; all their gray goes back  
Like water down a beach. Stand to the wall!—  
Nay, they are past my hurting, or my fear.  
*(He looks on the fallen soldier.)*  
Thou cam'st a mile of open to my musket,  
Poor valiant fool; what drove thee? Let me see  
What manner of face puts on such bravery;  
I'll give him water; dying asks no passport.  
*(He turns the body, and knows it.)*  
What dawn is this that rises in my eyes?  
Caleb. O God, it is my brother Caleb.  
These are my mother's eyes, made strange with smoke;  
This is the mouth that called me from the creek-bank.  
Speak, if thou hast a word in thee: 'tis Asa.  

**CALEB.**  
Asa? Then it is true, what the smoke whispered—  
I thought I knew the turning of thy shoulder  
Even as I came on, and could not stop:  
The charge was like a river, and I drowned in it.  
Thou wast but fifty paces, and a stranger's coat—  
I told thee, by the Rappahannock, brother:  
Thou'lt know me after.  

**ASA.**  
        Take my coat, my blood,  
Take both mine eyes, that were so quick to aim;  
O slow, slow hands, that were so swift to load!  
Where is the wound? Here? Let me staunch it. Live,  
And hate me fifty years, but live to do it.  

**CALEB.**  
No staunching this; thy steel was a good workman.  
Hark, ere the dark comes on. In my breast pocket—  
The letter, Mother's letter—it is wet,  
I fear 'tis wet—read it above my grave,  
And write to her; but Asa, on thy soul,  
Write that some stranger did it. Let her keep  
Two sons in memory, not one son and a horror.  

**ASA.**  
I'll write it; and the writing damn me, brother,  
For I am Cain with a government musket,  
And all this famous field one field of Nod.  

**CALEB.**  
No, hear me, for I see now very clear,  
The way men see at evening, the last furlong:  
Thou art no Cain; thou art a son of the house,  
And I am too; the house fell on us both.  
We drank of one milk, Asa; I remember;  
I kept the oath. Say to her... say the bands,  
That night upon the river... played one tune...  
The heron, Asa... we could never catch him...  
Yonder he rises from the swimming-hole...  
Going home... the slow wings... going home...  

*He dies.*  

**ASA.**  
And home is nowhere now, on either bank.  
*(He closes CALEB'S eyes, and takes the letter.)*  
O you that make the wars and write the songs,  
Come, stand you here, and look upon your work:  
One field, two coats, one blood, and that blood spilled  
By its own vintage. Men shall keep this ground  
Holy for ages, and raise marble here;  
But I will see, where'er the marble stands,  
A boy, a creek, a heron, and these hands.  
*(Soldiers return; a burying-party with spades.)*  
Friend, lend thy spade; this one is mine to bury.  
Ask me no more; or if thou must ask, write:  
Two brothers met, where brothers should not meet,  
And the survivor digs; and nothing more,  
Till the great muster-roll be called, is sure—  
Save this: such wars cost more than they restore.  

*Exeunt with the body. A dead march.*

## SCENE IV. Gettysburg. The new cemetery upon the hill, in November.

*Enter CHORUS.*  

**CHORUS.**  
Four months have swept the smoke from off the ridge;  
The summer's dead sleep now in ordered rows;  
And to the dedication of that sleep  
Come princes of the rostrum, hours of Greek,  
And, last and least by all the programme's promise,  
The tall sad man, with two small pages written.  
Hear now what fell, when, after the great orator  
Had spoken long and beautifully of nothing,  
The President stood up like a plain fact,  
And in the time a man might plough one furrow,  
Set words upon the field that shall outwear  
The marble, and the rostrum, and the age.  

*Exit. Enter LINCOLN, upon the platform; SEWARD, dignitaries, soldiers, and people, ASA among them, with crape on his sleeve.*  

**LINCOLN.**  
Fourscore and seven years are gathered up  
Since on this continent our fathers brought  
A new thing forth: a nation in conception  
Dedicate to a sentence—that all men  
Are made equal. Now a great civil war  
Tries in its furnace whether such a nation,  
So conceived and so sworn, can long endure.  
We meet upon a battle-field of that war;  
We come to give a portion of this field  
To be the last bed of the men who died  
That the nation might live. This we may fitly do;  
And yet, in a larger sense, we cannot give,  
We cannot hallow, cannot consecrate  
This ground: the brave, the living and the dead,  
Who struggled here, have hallowed it so far  
Above our poor addition or subtraction  
That the world will little note, nor long remember,  
What we say here, but never can forget  
What they did here. For us, the living, rather,  
To be here dedicated to the work  
Unfinished, which they fought here and advanced:  
That from these honoured dead we take increase  
Of devotion to that cause for which they gave  
The last full measure of devotion; that we here  
Highly resolve these dead shall not have died  
In vain; this nation, under God, shall have  
A new birth of freedom; and that government  
Of the people, by the people, for the people,  
Shall not perish from the earth.  

*A silence; then the people uncover.*  

**SEWARD.**  
        Sir, it was brief.  

**LINCOLN.**  
So is a prayer. Come; the day draws in.  

**ASA** *(coming forward, alone, as the rest depart)*.  
"These dead shall not have died in vain." Amen;  
And one of them wore gray, and he is in  
That bargain too, or I'll not sign it. Sleep,  
Caleb; the President has made it holy,  
And I have made it bitter, and the grass  
Will make it quiet, given a few Aprils.  
Now to the wars again, the long way home:  
For he said well: the work is *unfinished*; I,  
That have paid more to it than my whole life,  
Will see it finished, or be filed with these.  
On, soldier. Spring will find this hill again;  
Pray God it find as much in living men.  

*Exit.*

# ACT IV

## SCENE I. Washington. The White House.

*Enter LINCOLN and GRANT.*  

**LINCOLN.**  
So you are Grant. They told me you were small;  
You are the tallest man has held that door,  
For all your inches. Sit. You smoke? Smoke, then;  
This room has borne much worse vapours than tobacco.  
General, I'll be brief, and you be briefer:  
I have had generals in their gilded plenty—  
Engineers, courtiers, scholars of Napoleon—  
Who fought as if the object of the war  
Were to preserve their armies for parades.  
What is your plan?  

**GRANT.**  
        To find where Lee is, sir;  
To get at him as quick as the roads allow;  
To strike him hard as ever God gives leave;  
And keep on striking, moving by the left,  
Until it's done. Sherman the while goes south  
Through Georgia, at the larder and the loom.  
Two millstones, sir; the grinding will be ugly,  
And the bill long; I'll send it every week,  
Writ plain in casualties; and I will not stop.  

**LINCOLN.**  
At last. At last a man who'll take the arithmetic  
And not come asking me to mend the sums  
Each fortnight with fresh miracles. Hear me, Grant:  
The particulars of your plans I do not ask;  
I am sick of knowing better than my generals  
And being right too late. Hold Lee, and hammer;  
And if the hammering want more iron, write,  
And I will strip the very statehouse roofs.  

**GRANT.**  
There will be crying out, sir, at the cost.  

**LINCOLN.**  
There is crying now, and dying with no purchase.  
You'll be called butcher; I am called baboon;  
The graves are called by no name; that's the wrong of it,  
And only ending it can right the wrong.  
What is your headquarters?  

**GRANT.**  
        With the army, sir;  
In the field; wherever the next morning is.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Then there's my hand. I propose to be, myself,  
No further use to you than a good anchor  
Is to a captain: out of sight, and holding.  
Go to your work; and, general—  

**GRANT.**  
        Sir?  

**LINCOLN.**  
                Finish it.  
The land is tired in the bone, and I  
Am tired in some place deeper than the bone  
That has no surgeon. Finish it. Good night.  

*Exeunt severally.*  

---

## SCENE II. The trenches before Petersburg. Night.

*Enter FREEMAN, a sergeant of the United States Colored Troops, with a SQUAD; ASA, now a sergeant also, meeting him; POTTLE following with his pack.*  

**ASA.** Stand. The word, friend.  

**FREEMAN.** "Liberty," and the countersign "Union"; and I never gave a password yet with a better will.  

**ASA.** Pass, sergeant. *(peering)* Hold up thy lantern. I know that voice; it carried hay once, by Stoner creek. Cato?  

**FREEMAN.** It carried hay, and it carried Master Calhoun's humours, and a many things beside. But the name is Freeman now—I chose it from the whole dictionary, having at last the buying of a name—Sergeant Freeman, Fourth division, that stormed the crater. Asa Drummond! Give me thy hand. Nay, both.  

**ASA.** Both, and gladly. Thou art a sergeant before me in time, I'll warrant; how camest thou north?  

**FREEMAN.** By the underside of a hay-wagon, by a river at night, by the soles of my feet, and by the grace of God; the route is not in the gazetteer. And the day the Proclamation came, I stood in a camp at readiness, and a white colonel read it out in the rain, and no man minded the rain. Then they gave me a musket. Asa, I have studied on it since: a musket is a heavy thing, but it is the lightest thing that I have ever carried.  

**ASA.** *(aside)* There's the whole war in one man's baggage. *(to him)* And thy wife, that was sold into Tennessee?  

**FREEMAN.** I am looking for her, brother, with an army for my lantern. Every plantation that we pass, I ask. The men of my company all carry such questions, primed and loaded; we are a regiment of questions, and Richmond yonder is the answer-book. And thee? Thy mother? Thy brother Caleb—he went south, I mind it.  

**ASA.** *(after a pause)* Mother keeps the farm. Caleb keeps a hill in Pennsylvania.  

**FREEMAN.** ...So. I'll ask no further. The Lord comfort thee.  

**ASA.** He does what He can, I think, with the material.  

**POTTLE.** Gentlemen, gentlemen, a thought: it is cold, the night is long, the rebellion is dying but takes its time about it, like a rich uncle—and I have here a quart of what the invoice calls brandy.  

**ASA.** What does the truth call it?  

**POTTLE.** The truth, sergeant, was struck off the army list in sixty-one. One dollar.  

**FREEMAN.** I'll buy, sutler, and share it with this man, and thou shalt drink the first cup thyself, to prove thy wares.  

**POTTLE.** *(aside)* There is no trust left in this army. *(He drinks, and weeps a little.)* It is brandy after all; I am as surprised as any man.  

**FREEMAN.**  
Then pass it round; and here's the soldier's toast:  
*(He pours a little on the ground.)*  
That's for the dead, of both the wearing colours,  
Whom God now sorts by better marks than cloth.  
And this for the living: may the spring come green,  
The guns go home to be made ploughs and bells,  
And every man—I say it plain—*every*—  
Sit under his own vine, and fear no whip,  
And answer to a name himself has chosen.  

**ASA.**  
Amen, Sergeant Freeman. Thy name's well bought.  

**FREEMAN.**  
'Twas dear enough; two hundred years on credit.  
Come, walk the line with me; the night's half spent,  
And yonder in the dark, by Hatcher's Run,  
The last cold winter of the war sits coughing.  
It cannot live till May.  

**ASA.**  
        Pray God it cannot;  
I have a field at home wants better trenching.  

**FREEMAN.**  
And I a name wants using in the daylight.  
Soft, now; the parapet. And, Pottle—  

**POTTLE.**  
        Sergeant?  

**FREEMAN.**  
Keep thy head down; the Confederacy is dying,  
But it dies armed.  

**POTTLE.**  
        Sir, my head and I  
Have kept each other company four years  
By just that policy. After you, gentlemen:  
In all adventures of the bayonet,  
The house of Pottle follows—at some distance.  

*Exeunt along the trench.*  

---

## SCENE III. Washington. A chamber in the White House, by night.

*LINCOLN discovered, asleep in a chair; a single lamp. Enter to him, as in a dream, GHOSTS: a UNION SOLDIER, a CONFEDERATE SOLDIER, and the GHOST OF WILLIE, his son.*  

**UNION GHOST.**  
Father Abraham, we are thy three hundred thousand,  
And more; we came when thou didst call us. Sleep;  
We do not come for vengeance, but for company:  
Thou art more ours than any living man's.  

**CONFEDERATE GHOST.**  
And I am of the others, that thou never  
Didst call, yet always counted. From thy mouth  
We heard no "traitor" spoken like a curse.  
Judge not, thou saidst, that we be not judged.  
We heard it, in the smoke. The dead hold court  
More gently than the living; thou'rt acquitted there.  

**WILLIE'S GHOST.**  
Father, the white house thou must come to soon  
Is small, and quiet, and the rent is paid.  
Mother will weep; bid her not weep too long;  
There are no fevers here, and no elections.  

**LINCOLN** *(waking)*.  
Willie! Stay—nay, gone; only the lamp,  
And the long room, and the rain on the window-glass.  
*(He rises.)*  
This is the third night walking of that dream—  
Nay, not that dream; the other is worse: the ship,  
The same dark ship that sails before each battle,  
Bearing away to some indefinite shore;  
And then tonight, new-minted horror: a sound  
Of sobbing through the house; and I went down,  
And in the East Room, on a catafalque,  
A shape in funeral vestments, soldiers at watch,  
And all the mourners weeping. "Who is dead  
In the White House?" I asked a soldier. "The President,"  
Says he; "killed by an assassin." And the cry  
The people gave woke me, and here I am,  
Asking the dark what it is loath to tell.  

*Enter MARY LINCOLN, in a wrapper.*  

**MARY.**  
Husband, thou wert crying out.  

**LINCOLN.**  
        A dream, Mary;  
Only a dream; a President has dreams  
As a ship has barnacles; they slow the keel  
But do not steer it. Go to bed, my dear.  

**MARY.**  
Thou art telling me the half. I know that face;  
It is the face thou worest when Willie died,  
Schooled to be plain for my sake. Abraham,  
Promise me: when the city lights its windows  
For the surrender, go not out at night;  
There's many a pistol loose in a glad crowd,  
And thou art tall above all heads, and lit.  

**LINCOLN.**  
Mary, if any man would spend his life  
To purchase mine, no guard can quite forbid  
The bargain; and to live behind a shield,  
Peeping at my own people through a grate,  
Were to be dead already, and unburied.  
What God wills, ripens. I am watched enough.  
Come, I will tell thee a merrier thing: today  
Grant writes the lines at Petersburg wear thin,  
And Richmond cannot see another Sunday.  
Think on it: peace. I'll take thee to the play,  
We'll laugh at something foolish, like plain folk,  
And drive out to the Soldiers' Home in May  
When the dogwood comes. The war once done, sweet wife,  
I do intend to learn frivolity,  
Though I be the oldest pupil in the school.  

**MARY.**  
Then promise me the play, and a new bonnet,  
And no more dreams.  

**LINCOLN.**  
        The play, the bonnet—done.  
The dreams I cannot promise; they keep their own  
Appointments. Go along; I'll sit a little  
And hear the rain. *(Exit MARY.)*  
        "Killed by an assassin."  
Well, and if so? Death keeps no calendar  
That any man may read; and I have done  
The main of what I was, I think, sent for.  
Yet O, not yet; the knitting's at the needle's turn;  
Who else will bind these states up without malice?  
There's gentleness required now, and the men  
Of vengeance crowd my stairs. Lord, of Thy patience,  
A year. Give me one year of peace to spend  
As I have spent the war, and I'll come willing  
Aboard that ship, and ask not for the shore.  
*(He takes up his Bible.)*  
Rain, rain. The dogwood will be early though.  

*Exit with the lamp.*  

---

## SCENE IV. Appomattox Court House. A parlour.

*Enter GRANT, in a private soldier's blouse, mud upon his boots, with OFFICERS; to him LEE, in his best uniform, with a single AIDE.*  

**LEE.**  
General Grant.  

**GRANT.**  
        General Lee. We met before, sir,  
In Mexico; you would not remember it;  
I was a lieutenant, and the war was small.  

**LEE.**  
I have tried, sir, to remember, and cannot;  
Forgive an old man's memory; it keeps  
What it can bear. You know why I am come.  

**GRANT.**  
I do; and I would have you know, my sword  
Is not in this room, nor shall yours be asked for.  
Sir, write it as I say it: the men paroled,  
Each officer to keep his side-arms, horse,  
And personal baggage; and each man of yours  
That claims a horse or mule may take it home—  
They'll want them for the spring ploughing. Furthermore,  
Your army is hungry; mine has rations; Sheridan,  
See twenty-five thousand issued before night.  

**LEE.**  
This will do much, sir, toward the conciliating  
Of all our people. It is generous;  
More than I looked for. *(He signs.)*  
        It is strange: four years  
I have led these men past hunger, and past hope,  
Till they and I forgot there was an ending;  
And now the ending is a quiet parlour,  
A table, and a very courteous enemy,  
And outside, April. General, your terms  
Do honour to your nation, and to you;  
And he that shows such mercy in his morning  
Will not be hard remembered in the evening.  

**GRANT.**  
Sir, there is no rejoicing in my camp;  
I have forbid the batteries their salutes.  
The rebels are our countrymen again,  
And we shall want their shoulders at the wheel.  
The war is done; let no man cheer a funeral.  

**LEE.**  
The war is done. *(aside)* And what am I, that said  
Virginia is the fire upon the hearth?  
The hearth is ash, the roof-tree is brought low,  
And half the boys that cheered me on the road  
Are bivouacked where no reveille can reach.  
Was the love wrong, that chose? I cannot tell;  
I am too near; let after-ages judge,  
That see both slopes of every mountain. This  
I know: I am tired past any field's repairing,  
And there is yet one duty: to go out  
To those thin ranks, and bid them plant their corn,  
And neither nurse the war about their hearths  
Nor be ashamed of valour. After that,  
Some quiet college, and the mending trade:  
I have unmade enough young men; I'll spend  
What evening I am given, making some.  

**GRANT.**  
Your horse is at the door, sir.  

**LEE.**  
        Gentlemen.  

*The officers uncover as he goes. Exit LEE.*  

**GRANT.**  
There goes the last of an old world, gentlemen,  
Sitting his gray horse like a monument  
That has dismounted from its pedestal  
To ride away into the history books.  
Uncover, and no cheering. Send the wires:  
"General Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia  
This afternoon, on terms proposed." No more;  
Victories write short. Come in; the rain begins;  
And pray we manage peace, now we have won it,  
With half the patience that we spent on war:  
It is the harder trade of the two, by far.  

*Exeunt.*

# ACT V

## SCENE I. Washington. Before the Capitol; the fourth of March. A great crowd.

*Enter LINCOLN, to take the oath; SEWARD, STANTON, justices, soldiers, and people; among the crowd, FREDERICK DOUGLASS; and apart, above, JOHN WILKES BOOTH.*  

**LINCOLN.**  
Fellow-countrymen: at this second appearing  
There is less occasion for an extended speech.  
Four years ago, all thoughts were anxiously  
Bent on a coming war; all dreaded it,  
All sought to avert it; and the war came. One eighth  
Of this whole people were not people, by law,  
But property, and somehow, all men knew,  
That property was the cause. Neither side looked  
For the war's magnitude, nor its duration;  
Each looked for easier triumph. Both, mark this,  
Both read the same Bible, and both pray  
To the same God, and each invokes His aid  
Against the other. It may seem most strange  
That any men dare ask a just God's help  
In wringing out their bread from others' sweat;  
But judge not, that we be not judged. The prayers  
Of both could not be answered; neither's prayer  
Was answered fully, for the Almighty has  
His own purposes. If we suppose this war  
Is but the woe due to that vast offence,  
Two hundred years of bondage, and that He  
Gives North and South together this terrible war  
As the offence's wage—shall we discern  
Any departure from those attributes  
Believers in a living God ascribe Him?  
Fondly do we hope, fervently do we pray,  
This mighty scourge of war may pass away;  
Yet if God wills that it continue till  
All wealth piled up by bondsmen's unpaid toil  
Be sunk, and every drop of blood the lash drew  
Be paid by one drawn with the sword, still must it  
Be said, as was said thirty centuries gone,  
The judgments of the Lord are true and righteous  
Altogether.  
        With malice toward none;  
With charity for all; with firmness in  
The right, as God gives us to see the right—  
Let us strive on, to finish up the work  
We are in; to bind the nation's wounds; to care  
For him who shall have borne the battle, and for  
His widow, and his orphan; and to do all  
Which may achieve and cherish a just peace,  
A lasting peace, among ourselves, and with  
All nations.  

*The oath is administered; guns; the people shout.*  

**DOUGLASS** *(in the crowd)*.  
        That was a sermon, not a speech;  
There has not stood upon this continent  
A more high pulpit, nor a humbler preacher.  
I'll tell him so tonight, though ushers frown:  
"Sir, it was a sacred effort." Mark the phrase,  
For history will be needing it.  

**BOOTH** *(apart, above)*.  
        Mark *me*.  
I stood this close. I might have—patience, patience.  
What a deal of weather: God, and blood, and judgment;  
The nigh-crowned Caesar drizzling scripture on  
The mob that crowns him. And they cheered—dear God,  
The cheering. There stood I, a Brutus pinioned  
In a crowd of Casca's betters, and did nothing.  
Well. Ides have come before, and come again;  
The play is cast; I wait but on the cue.  

*Exeunt all severally; BOOTH last, looking after LINCOLN.*  

---

## SCENE II. Washington. A mean room at a boarding-house.

*Enter BOOTH, POWELL, ATZERODT, and HEROLD, with a map of the city.*  

**BOOTH.**  
Sit, gentlemen—I use the word as charity.  
The plot is altered. Kidnapping is dead;  
That was a comedy, and the times are tragic.  
Richmond is fallen, did you hear them sing it?  
Fallen; and last night, at his window, *he*,  
The ape in the black suit, the rail-splitting Caesar,  
Spoke of the vote for *negroes*. That was his cue,  
And mine. The knife now, gentlemen, the pistol:  
Three doors, one night, one hour. Powell, the Secretary,  
Seward, sick in his bed; Atzerodt, the Vice,  
Johnson, at his hotel; the principal role  
Is mine by right of blood and of profession:  
I play the President.  

**ATZERODT.**  
        I undertook  
A stealing, Master Booth, a carrying-off;  
I never bargained murder. I am a ferryman;  
I know the river's moods, not this.  

**BOOTH.**  
        Thou'lt do it,  
Or hang with us for the conspiracy  
With nothing done: the rope is the same length.  
Choose thy part: actor, or only property.  

**POWELL.**  
Give me the door and the knife; I ask no lines.  

**HEROLD.**  
And I'll hold horses, and show Powell the streets.  

**BOOTH.**  
Why, there's a cast. Tomorrow night, the tenth hour;  
Synchronize; strike; and southward through the dark  
To Maryland, where friends and fresh horses wait.  
Go, learn your doors. *(Exeunt POWELL, ATZERODT, HEROLD.)*  
        Now sits the scene alone,  
And I may drop the manager, and soliloquize.  
They'll write me mad. I am not mad; I am *exact*.  
My father christened me for one that struck  
A grasping tribune down—Junius his line—  
And all my life upon the boards I have mouthed  
Other men's thunder, in the second part:  
My brother got the Hamlets, I the fencing.  
But now comes on the scene that none shall play  
But I, before the widest house in the world,  
And after it, no actor shall be named  
But men shall say, "Ay, but did you see Booth?"  
The South is fallen; good; her ghost shall thank me;  
I do it for Virginia—and that's half;  
The other half, I'll own here in the dark:  
For glory; for the bill-boards of all time.  
"Sic semper tyrannis": there's my single line;  
Caesar had half a hundred daggers; mine,  
One little derringer shall out-write them all.  
He goes to the play on Friday. Good. 'Tis fit:  
He loved the theatre that ne'er loved him—  
One actor loved him not, and proves it—*scene*:  
The box, the bracket, and the bar of pine,  
I have walked the stage of Ford's since I could walk;  
I know its every trap and breath; the leap  
Is twelve feet to the boards, a player's leap,  
And then the lights, the horse, the dark, the legend.  
Some shall cry parricide, and some, deliverer;  
Let them cry on; the curtain knows its hour;  
And History, that old reviewer, writes  
At last a notice that shall outlast Booth—  
No—that shall *be* Booth: I'll be all they read.  
How fearful a thing it is, and how delicious,  
To know the playbill of the age by heart  
A week before the age. Sleep now, John Wilkes;  
Thou hast rehearsed enough; the house fills Friday;  
And one shall take his last of mortal bows  
That never trod a stage, nor needed one.  

*Exit.*  

---

## SCENE III. Ford's Theatre. The night of Good Friday.

*The stage of the theatre seen, and above it the President's box, dressed with flags. Enter, to the box, LINCOLN, MARY, MAJOR RATHBONE, and his lady; the house rises, with music; they sit. Below, PLAYERS perform a comedy.*  

**MARY.**  
What will Miss Harris think of me, husband,  
Hanging upon your hand so?  

**LINCOLN.**  
        Why, she'll think  
Nothing of it at all. Let the hand stay.  
*(The players jest below; the house laughs.)*  

**MARY.**  
You laughed outright; I have not heard that laugh,  
I think, since Springfield.  

**LINCOLN.**  
        It is the new peace, Mary;  
Laughter is one of the discharged soldiers' arts;  
It will come home by regiments this summer.  
I thought today of what we two might do  
When the term's done: we'll see the Holy Land,  
They say Jerusalem is—  

*Enter, behind, to the door of the box, BOOTH; he watches the play below for his moment.*  

**BOOTH** *(aside)*.  
        Now the house empties  
Of all but one player on the stage; the line—  
The laughing line—comes now; and the great laugh  
Shall be my cover. Steady, hand; thou art  
The hand of all the South tonight—so cast,  
So billed. The cue. *(The house laughs loud.)*  

*BOOTH fires. LINCOLN'S head falls. MARY shrieks. RATHBONE grapples with BOOTH, who slashes him and leaps from the box to the stage below.*  

**BOOTH** *(upon the stage)*.  
Sic semper tyrannis! The South is avenged!  

*He flies. Tumult; the house in uproar.*  

**RATHBONE.**  
Stop that man! Stop him! The President is shot!  

**MARY.**  
O God, his head—help—is there a doctor—help me—  
Abraham—husband—speak; thou spok'st but now;  
The sentence is not finished—finish it—  
Jerusalem—we were to see Jerusalem—  

**A SURGEON** *(in the box)*.  
Lay him along. The wound is mortal, madam.  
He will not wake. We'll bear him over the street;  
He must not die in a playhouse.  

**MARY.**  
        Nor anywhere!  
The war is over, sirs, the war is over,  
They lit the city for it, every window—  
O, all the candles, and put out this one?  

*They take up LINCOLN. The players and people kneel as he is borne through. A drum begins to beat slowly.*

## SCENE IV. A small back chamber in the Petersen house; LINCOLN laid diagonally upon a bed too short for him. STANTON, SEWARD'S messenger, SURGEONS, about him; MARY without, attended. Rain heard. It is near morning.

*Enter STANTON, from the bedside, to write dispatches; a SURGEON attending.*  

**STANTON.**  
What is the hour?  

**SURGEON.**  
        Past seven, and full daylight,  
Though you would never know it by the sky.  
The breathing fails, sir.  

**STANTON.**  
        I have sent the wires  
All night, like a man bailing out the sea:  
Troops here, the bridges watched, the murderer named—  
An actor; Booth; the play-bills knew his face  
Better than all my marshals. Now it slacks,  
The bailing, for the flood has won. Look there:  
The long body, that bore the longest burden  
Since Washington, aslant a hireling bed,  
And nothing more for any man to do  
But stand, and be diminished by the watching.  
I fought him, do you know, when first we met—  
I called him ape, gorilla, in my courtroom days;  
And he, when time had taught me what he was,  
Forgave so quietly I never knew  
The hour of my pardon. There was in that man  
Something I have no secretary's word for:  
He was the shortest distance between justice  
And mercy that this country ever drew.  

**SURGEON** *(at the bed)*.  
Sir. It is over.  

*A silence. The watchers kneel. A PARSON prays softly within.*  

**STANTON.**  
        Now he belongs to the ages.  
*(He covers his face a moment; then:)*  
Up, gentlemen. Grief is a private soldier;  
Duty's the officer. The government  
Of the United States lives, and this morning  
Will be about its business, as he'd have it.  
Send for the Vice-President; sound the bells;  
And let the trains bear him to Illinois  
Slowly, by the long way, through all the towns,  
That the plain people, that he never doubted,  
May stand along the cuttings with their hats off.  
He was their kind; let them have time to see him.  

*Exeunt with the body, borne; the bells begin. Manent CHORUS-wise, meeting in the street without: DOUGLASS, ASA, and FREEMAN, the rain ending.*  

**ASA.**  
Sergeant. Mr. Douglass. So the bells are true;  
I hoped all night the wires had learned to lie.  

**FREEMAN.**  
True as a grave. I heard it at the camp;  
The men of my regiment stood out in the rain  
And no one spoke; we had no word for it;  
We had used up our weeping years ago  
On lesser griefs—and yet there was rain enough  
On every face.  

**DOUGLASS.**  
        I knew him. He was the first  
Great man I talked with in this land that never—  
Not once, not in a gesture, nor a pause—  
Remembered me my colour to my face.  
The deed of Friday night, let no man think it  
Was one mad actor's: it was slavery's self,  
Striking, in its death-spasm, at the hand  
That signed its death. The serpent's head is crushed  
And the tail kills. Well: we will bury him,  
And keep the thing he signed; that is the mourning  
That he would set us: keep it; *make it good*;  
For parchment promises, like April ice,  
Bear no man's weight until they're proved and proved.  

**ASA.**  
I am for home; my musket's out of date.  
There is a farm in Bourbon county, sirs,  
Two graves in it of late, and one old woman,  
And fields gone shaggy that my brother loved.  
I'll plough them in his name. Sergeant—thy wife?  

**FREEMAN.**  
Found. In a contraband camp by Nashville: found.  
She did not know me in the uniform;  
And then she knew me. Brother, I'll not try  
To say that morning; there's no saying it;  
But it is morning still, for all this bell.  
I keep the army a year more; then a school—  
They are crying out for teachers in the south  
Who know their letters and have seen the elephant;  
I know my letters; my whole life's the lesson.  

**DOUGLASS.**  
Then go, the both: the ploughman and the teacher;  
You are the peace; be better than the war.  
And I'll go speak, while there is breath to spend,  
That what the dead man called the unfinished work  
Stay live in the land's conscience like a thorn—  
A blessèd thorn, that lets no easy sleeping.  
Gentlemen, your hands. So. North and South,  
And the third hand that neither side would shake  
Till it had bled for both. Here's the new Union,  
Standing in one wet street. Let it hold fast.  
The bells. Come, walk with me toward the bells.  

*Exeunt together.*

## EPILOGUE

*Enter CHORUS.*  

**CHORUS.**  
Our play is done; the players go to supper;  
The dead arise, washed clean of mimic blood—  
Would the four years had risen half so kindly.  
A little month, and Booth, run down at last,  
Burned in a barn like vermin, his last word  
"Useless"; let that be all his monument.  
The South came home; the grass came back; the graves  
Sank slowly level, as the heart will too.  
The tall man sleeps in Springfield, with his Willie;  
And what he was grows larger every year,  
As mountains do, the further the road runs.  
But you that have sat gently through our scenes,  
Take this much homeward from the fable: war,  
Though it wear glory like a scarlet coat,  
Is, underneath, two brothers and a field;  
And union, though the lawyers prove it parchment,  
Is, underneath, three mourners in a street  
Who hold their hands out, having buried much.  
The house divided fell, and was rebuilt;  
The mason's bill was paid in blood and guilt;  
And still 'tis building; still the courses rise;  
Each age must mortar it, or see it fall;  
With malice toward none, with charity for all.  

*Exit.*  

**FINIS.**
